But since then she's praised Trump and RFK... not really the energy we need in statewide office in California.

Proposition 37: Creates Loan Program for Middle-Income Buyers of Qualified New Homes

YES
  • Creates a program to help certain qualified Californians receive downpayment assistance when buying a home.
  • At first, this looks like a demand subsidy for housing which would do little to address housing affordability, but looking closer it's actually an incentive mechanism for building new housing: the government loans can only be used on new homes. So developers may be incentivized to build more as a result because it essentially expands the pool of who might be able to buy the homes they're building.
  • I'm probably voting yes on anything with even a little potential to create more housing, and this fits that bill.

Proposition 40: Billionaire Tax

NO
  • I am conflicted here because, yes, billionaires do not pay enough taxes. But the authors of this proposal essentially managed to make every possible mistake in drafting it and end up with a wealth tax that's deeply flawed.
  • Some better ideas for how to tax billionaires:
    • Repeal or at least reform 1978 prop 13. The Los Angeles Country Club does not to continue to receive a massive taxpayer subsidy.
    • Tax equity-collateralized loans. This is essentially a loophole that allows billionaires to realize their gains without ever technically selling equity and incurring capital-gains taxes buy borrowing against their holdings and only paying interest.

Proposition 41: Poison pill for billionaire tax

NO
  • This is basically a confusing audit requirement that would have little actual effect except to prevent prop 40 from going into effect if both passed and this one earns more YES votes. That's not really how democracy should work. Even though I disagree with prop 40, as a matter of principle "poison pill" ballot measures like this are an awful way to set policy.

Proposition 42: Another poison pill for the billionaire tax

NO
  • Did opponents of prop 40 really need two separate poison pills? This one highlights that the tax woudld be retroactive, which is one of the reasons I oppose it (see my prop 40 argument), but once again poison pills are a bad idea. A simple ballot argument mentioning retroactivity being a bad idea would have been fine, but for some reason prop 40 detractors want to waste everyone's time and taxpayer money on making ballots and election guides longer.
  • Whether prop 40 passes should be based on how voters vote on prop 40, not a bunch of unrelated confusingly worded measures.

Illustrative Mockup — Not a Real Booking Site

Purpose of this file: This is a demonstration built to illustrate a consumer-protection concern with the proposed DOT rule that would let airlines display a fee-exclusive price with equal visual prominence to the fee-inclusive price. The red annotation boxes below call out design choices (large discounted-looking headline number, fine-print fee disclosure, no visible total until late in the flow) that a bad-faith actor could use to make the fee-exclusive figure functionally dominant even though it complies with a "no larger font" requirement on paper. This is meant for policy analysis, public comment, or educational discussion of drip-pricing and dark-pattern risks — not as a template for an actual product.
Both "$89" and "$214" are rendered at exactly 42px — identical font-size, satisfying a rule that only regulates size. The fee-inclusive total is de-emphasized purely through font-weight (300 vs 800), color (light gray vs saturated green), and by trailing after the headline number.

Both prices on this page are set at the identical 42px font-size — literally compliant with a rule that only restricts font size. Prominence is instead controlled by font-weight (800 vs 300), color saturation and contrast (green vs light gray on white, near WCAG-failing contrast for the total), reading order (base fare first, total second), and absence of any visual container around the total. This illustrates why a font-size-only prominence rule can be circumvented without violating its text, and why regulators/commenters may want language covering weight, color contrast, and placement as well.
